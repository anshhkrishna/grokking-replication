Trained a small transformer from scratch on modular addition at three weight decay values, 0, 0.3, and 1.0, three seeds each, holding architecture, data split, and step budget fixed. At zero weight decay, final test accuracy averaged 67.5% across seeds, ranging from 35.8% to 85.5%, and never crossed the 99% generalization threshold. At weight decay 0.3 and 1.0, final test accuracy averaged 96.9% and 96.5%, and every single nonzero weight decay seed beat every zero weight decay seed, a clean separation with no overlap. The one genuinely surprising part is that weight decay 1.0 was not a fast, reliable generalizer once more seeds were run: a first single seed crossed the 99% threshold cleanly with no memorization to generalization gap, but a second seed's test accuracy climbed to 92.5%, dropped back to 49.3%, and only then recovered, while a third never crossed the threshold at all despite ending near the same accuracy as the others. So the direction of the claim held, weight decay helps generalization at a fixed step budget, but the specific picture of a threshold above which grokking arrives quickly and predictably did not survive contact with more seeds.
